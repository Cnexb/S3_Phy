JPHG02.4 Question 1

Jimmy uses a joulemeter to find the specific heat capacity of water.

  • Mass of water = 0.2 kg
  • Initial temperature = 25 °C
  • Final temperature = 42 °C
  • Initial joulemeter reading = 64 350 J
  • Final joulemeter reading = 79 470 J

What is the specific heat capacity of water?

  • A. 1 800 J kg−1 °C−1
  • B. 3 020 J kg−1 °C−1
  • C. 4 450 J kg−1 °C−1
  • D. 23 400 J kg−1 °C−1

JPHG02.5 Question 2

A heater supplies 1000 J of energy to a 5 kg metal block. The temperature of the metal block rises from 25 °C to 35 °C. Assume that there is no energy loss to the surroundings. What is the heat capacity of the metal block?

  • A. 20 J °C−1
  • B. 50 J °C−1
  • C. 100 J °C−1
  • D. 200 J °C−1

JPHG02.6 Question 3

A metal block of mass 0.1 kg at 150 °C is put in water of mass 1 kg at 20 °C. The heat capacity of the metal block is 100 J °C−1. What is the final temperature of the mixture?

Specific heat capacity of water = 4200 J kg−1 °C−1

  • A. 20.3 °C
  • B. 23.0 °C
  • C. 85 °C
  • D. Cannot be determined

JPHG02 Heat and internal energy — Quiz 2 · Answer key

Teacher page · 3 items

JPHG02.4 Question 1

Answer: C. 4 450 J kg−1 °C−1

Guide: Energy supplied = 79 470 − 64 350 = 15 120 J. ΔT = 42 °C − 25 °C = 17 °C. c = 15 120 / (0.2 × 17) = 4 450 J kg−1 °C−1.

JPHG02.5 Question 2

Answer: C. 100 J °C−1

Guide: C = Q / ΔT = 1000 J / (35 °C − 25 °C) = 100 J °C−1.

JPHG02.6 Question 3

Answer: B. 23.0 °C

Guide: Energy lost by the block equals energy gained by the water: 100 × (150 − T) = 1 × 4200 × (T − 20), so T = 23.0 °C.
