Check the tool against these. If the tool’s number or direction disagrees, the tool is wrong.

## A1

(a) Φ = BA cos 0° = 0.40 × 0.020 × 1 = **0.0080 Wb**.

(b) Φ = 0.40 × 0.020 × cos 60° = 0.0080 × 0.5 = **0.0040 Wb**.

Turning toward 90° reduces the flux. It does not increase it.

## A2

Flux linkage falls from NΦ = 200 × 0.0080 = 1.6 Wb to zero.

|ε| = N |ΔΦ| / Δt = 1.6 / 0.050 = **32 V**.

## A3

(a) |ε| = 1.6 / 0.10 = **16 V**.

(b) The flux change is the same, but it takes longer, so |d(NΦ)/dt| is smaller.

## A4

(a) **North pole.** Lenz’s law: the induced current opposes the increase of flux into the coil, so the near face repels the approaching north pole.

(b) **South pole.** The flux into the coil is now decreasing. The induced current opposes that decrease, so the near face attracts the leaving north pole.

The direction reverses when the motion reverses. “The coil always attracts” is wrong.

## A5

The student is **wrong**. Faraday’s law needs a **changing** flux linkage. A steady field and a stationary magnet give dΦ/dt = 0, so the induced emf is zero.
