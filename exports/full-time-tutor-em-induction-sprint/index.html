Part one · 02 · The task

Build a browser tool that teaches electromagnetic induction. The student must be able to change something, commit to an answer, and be told whether it is right and why.

DSE Physics Topic: Electromagnetic induction · set Deliverable: HTML

An interactive tool for Electromagnetic induction.

Teach one idea from SPEM07: magnetic flux Φ = BA cos θ, Faraday’s law ε = −dΦ/dt, and Lenz’s law for direction. The student changes quantities, predicts, then sees whether they are right and why.

Learning goal

After using your tool, a student should be able to find flux and induced emf for a simple change, and use Lenz’s law for the direction — and be corrected by the tool when they get it wrong.

◈ The topic is set, the teaching is yours Electromagnetic induction is fixed. How you teach it — flux sliders, a moving magnet, a changing loop — is your decision.

Build one of these

  • SuggestedA flux explorer: the student sets B, A and θ, predicts Φ or the induced emf when one quantity changes, then sees the result and the reason.
  • AlternativeA magnet and coil: the student changes speed or which pole enters first, predicts the current direction with Lenz’s law, and is told why.
  • AlternativeA changing loop: the area grows or shrinks in a steady field. The student predicts the emf before the tool reveals it.

Your tool must include

  • At least two things the student can change, not just a Next button.
  • A point where the student commits to an answer before seeing the result.
  • Feedback that says right or wrong and explains why, in plain English.
  • A worked example button, and a Try another button with fresh numbers.
  • One HTML file, no external libraries, readable on a laptop. Add Traditional Chinese labels beside the English where it helps a DSE student.

TOPIC (set): Electromagnetic induction (HKDSE SPEM07) — magnetic flux Φ = BA cos θ, Faraday’s law ε = −dΦ/dt, and Lenz’s law for the direction of the induced current. Do not confuse this with the force on a current-carrying wire (Fleming’s left-hand rule).

Read every file in the folder task_em_induction_interactive_tool of this project — the teaching brief and the practice set — and follow the way flux, Faraday and Lenz are taught there. Then create a single-file HTML teaching tool for a Hong Kong DSE physics student on the topic above. The tool must: (1) let the student change at least two things, for example B and A, or speed and pole; (2) ask the student for an answer or a prediction before it shows anything; (3) say clearly whether they are right or wrong AND explain why in one or two sentences; (4) have a “Show worked example” button; (5) have a “Try another” button that generates a fresh question with clean numbers. Use large readable text, keep the same colour for the same quantity throughout, write in plain English for a 16-year-old with Traditional Chinese labels beside the key terms, and use no external libraries.
